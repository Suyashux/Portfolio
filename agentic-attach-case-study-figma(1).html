01 / Overview

Help people buy the setup, not just the product.

Ask Blue is Best Buy’s conversational shopping assistant. Agentic Attach, internally “Guided Completion,” brings relevant accessories into the product decision so customers can understand what completes their setup before they commit.

My contribution

I led the end-to-end design, from problem framing and PRD collaboration through interaction design, content strategy, and the confidence-based recommendation framework that Product, Engineering, and Data Science could build against.

  • Turned a fuzzy compatibility problem into a shippable MVP scope.
  • Defined confidence-tiered logic for when, and when not, to show accessories.
  • Designed the conversational journey across compare, product detail, reviews, and Add to Cart.
  • Co-authored UX requirements, success metrics, and fallback behavior with Product.
02 / Problem

Customers realize what’s missing too late.

The existing attach experience was an easy-to-miss prompt and a mixed compatibility list, rather than a structured part of the purchase decision.

“Customers don’t know what they need — until it’s too late.”

What customers experience

  • They lack a reference for what people in similar situations typically buy.
  • They see complementary products only after purchase.
  • They underestimate how much accessories improve the experience.
  • They struggle to connect a product to the outcome they want.
  • They discover required setup items too late.

Why the current experience falls short

  • A mixed list makes accessory types hard to distinguish.
  • Missing category context makes options difficult to compare.
  • Attach feels like an afterthought, not part of the decision.
  • The result is incomplete purchases, setup friction, and lower confidence.

03 / Goals

Make attach easier to notice, understand, and trust.

The hypothesis: surface relevant accessories when customers show product interest in chat, during comparison and evaluation, and at Add to Cart. Timely, confidence-aware suggestions can help customers understand what completes their setup and improve attach rate and AOV without eroding trust.

Goal 01

Increase attach rate & AOV

Make relevant accessories easier to discover during product evaluation and at Add to Cart.

Goal 02

Build purchase confidence

Clearly present available options so customers do not miss what they need to complete a setup.

Goal 03

Establish a scalable foundation

Build a confidence-aware pattern for accessory discovery across the conversational shopping journey.

Success measures

Primary: attach rate, the share of orders with at least one additional item. Secondary: average order value and multi-item add-to-cart rate. Supporting: category-level performance versus PDP attach, plus category engagement, drill-down, and product interaction.

V1 scope discipline

Accessories only · Prompts during product interest and evaluation, plus Add to Cart · Confidence-gated recommendations · Product and category context kept clear. Future opportunities: individual-history personalization, complete-my-setup bundles, broader catalog coverage, and additional entry points.

04 / Approach

Gate relevance, not just quantity.

Compatibility data already existed. The design gap was deciding when that data was strong enough to act on. I reframed the question from “What can we show?” to “Do we have permission to speak up?”

01 / UnderstandProduct + intent

What is the customer evaluating or trying to complete?

→
02 / EvaluateEvidence quality

Co-purchase data, product specifications, and business rules.

→
03 / GateConfidence tier

Is compatibility confirmed, suggested, or too uncertain?

→
04 / RespondShow, qualify, or hold

Choose a response that protects relevance and trust.

High confidence · Recommend

Organize the complete setup

For a clear need and strong compatibility signal, group the most impactful accessories into useful categories. Explain why each matters and how the pieces work together.

Evidence: co-purchase + specifications + business rules
Mid confidence · Qualify

Offer, don’t guarantee

When co-purchase data suggests relevance but compatibility is not confirmed, present optional or commonly bought-together pairings without implying guaranteed fit.

Language: optional / commonly paired
Low or no confidence · Hold

Nothing forced

When confidence is low or absent, do not show an accessory suggestion. Keep the conversation and primary task open.

Fallback: no speculative attach

The confidence framework is the interface: it defines not only what the assistant says, but when it should stay quiet.

05 / Journey

An ambient thread through evaluation.

Attach is not a destination screen. It appears at meaningful moments in the evaluation loop, so people can discover what complements a product without abandoning the task they came to do.

CompareOffer relevant accessories when comparison shows product interest.
Read reviewsUse review questions as a natural prompt to explore a complete setup.
Explore setupMatch the recommendation format to compatibility confidence.
Add to CartPrimary attach surface, supported by earlier discovery moments.
Moment 01 · Compare

Open attach from product comparison

A relevant follow-up invites accessory discovery without interrupting the customer’s comparison task.

Moment 02 · Reviews

Carry intent forward from reviews

After the customer explores review themes, a low-friction prompt keeps the conversation moving toward a complete setup.

Moment 03 · High confidence

Group compatible items by setup need

When compatibility is well supported, organize recommendations into categories such as better sound, installation and mounting, and TV connections. Explain why each category helps.

Moment 03 · Medium confidence

Suggest common pairings, not guaranteed fit

When purchase patterns suggest relevance but compatibility is not confirmed, show a small optional set. Explain that customers may not need every item and invite them to choose what to explore.

06 / Add to Cart

The primary surface gets the clearest rules.

Attach can enter the conversation as soon as a customer shows interest in a product. Add to Cart is the primary surface for a focused recommendation, building on earlier prompts during comparison, product exploration, and reviews without interrupting the purchase confirmation.

Primary surface
At Add to Cart

These three states apply the same confidence model at the primary attach moment: a single strong signal earns one clear offer; high confidence earns organized accessory choices; low confidence earns no recommendation. Earlier product-interest prompts use the same relevance and confidence rules.

State 01 · Single strong signal

Confirm, then offer one thing

For a non-member who adds a TV, lead with one relevant protection-plan recommendation. Keep the value clear and the conversation focused.

State 02 · High confidence

Complete the setup

Once the TV and protection plan are in cart, show one accessory carousel across soundbars, wall mounts, and cables, with follow-ups to explore the best-fit category.

State 03 · Low confidence

Confirm; don’t fill the space

When nothing clears the confidence bar, simply confirm the purchase and reopen the conversation. Do not invent a recommendation to occupy the moment.

07 / System

Build a pattern, not a one-off page.

Attach spans comparison, product exploration, reviews, and Add to Cart. I designed shared conversational building blocks so each product-interest moment can offer a consistent, confidence-aware path to a more complete setup.

01Category blocks

Structured groups give shoppers context for comparison.

02Product cards

Consistent product details and actions inside conversation.

03Prompt pills

Suggested next steps keep exploration moving naturally.

04Response feedback

A shared pattern for improving the assistant's answers.

08 / Roadmap

Start focused. Earn the next phase.

The MVP is intentionally small: ship the foundation, test the hypothesis, and use evidence to earn broader investment.

Now · MVP

Ship the foundation

  • Accessory discovery during product interest and at Add to Cart
  • Confidence-gated recommendations and category structure
  • One primary product context at a time
Next

Widen the aperture

  • Medium-confidence optional pairings
  • More category exploration beyond the initial suggestions
  • Additional relevant moments across the shopping journey
Later

Personalize and extend

  • Personalization from customer context and history
  • Intent-aware, conversation-driven attach
  • ML-driven ranking and lifecycle attach

This seeds “Complete My Setup”: help customers succeed with what they bought. Existing compatibility data makes this a natural evolution of the platform, not a net-new capability.
